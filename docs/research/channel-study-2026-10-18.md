our program was missing into skills and features that the video-making process actually uses. Method: each channel's
page (latest and Popular videos with durations and views) and the YouTube storyboard contact sheet of one typical video
(a frame every 2-10 s across the opening and the middle), read in the app's browser. Transcripts could not be read
(YouTube blocks them without a signed-in player), so narration notes come from the titles, the structure on the
contact sheets and the channels' well-known formats. Techniques only: never a channel's look, words, voice or identity

## What they share

1. **The title promises, the first seconds prove**: a hidden truth or a counted list, restated as a question over the
   strongest picture within 5-15 s.
2. **One visual system per video**: a recurring world, map, ground or set; cutaways in the same palette.
3. **One accent with a meaning** (the hero, the clue, the side).
4. **Receipts on screen**: documents and headlines with the line that matters, quote cards with the speaker, charts of
   the numbers said, maps for every place.
5. **Maps carry geopolitics**, often with networks of people and countries on them.
6. **Pace by format**: explainers and lists change picture every 3-5 s; story documentaries every 5-10 s with motion
   inside; mysteries hold 6-10 s; music films cut several times every 5 s.
7. **One narration mode per channel**: curious explainer, first-person investigator, dramatic storyteller, direct list
   coach, evidence teacher, dry historian, calm mystery narrator.
8. **Length follows the story**: explainers 8-30 min, story documentaries 20-60 min, lists 12-20 min, deep mysteries
   30 min to over 2 h, short films 2-6 min.

## What our program was missing, and what 0.39 adds

| Missing | Added |
|---|---|
| Quote cards with the speaker | `quote` element: portrait in the cast look, name tag, words arriving at a speaking pace, key words lit |
| Documents and headlines as evidence | `document`: newspaper, letter, report or post; headline; one line that lights up; never a real outlet's logo |
| A dial for "item 3 of 7" and for readings | `gauge` with steps or a value, needle leaning past its mark and settling |
| Two scales at once (the clue magnified) | `loupe` |
| A measured distance | `ruler` (range line) |
| "1 in 100" as something to count | chart type `units` |
| Redacted files | text animation `redact` |
| Era looks for history and mystery (archive, newsprint, noir, deco) | the 14 film looks |
| Skills that never reached a video (349 drafts, none approved, zero uses) | pack approval in one named review, skills sent to the scene planner, the story director and voice direction, and recorded per job |
| The study itself as guidance | `skills/channel-grammar.json`: 11 general skills and 12 channel-like profiles that reach only a family they are pinned to |

Live footage (maximise, kold, Gawx) cannot be filmed by the program; their structure and cutting rhythm are kept as
profiles for footage-based documentary styles.
